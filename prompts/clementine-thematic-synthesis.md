# Thematic synthesis protocol

This sitting is a research production task, not a memo. Preserve interpretive synthesis. Wrap it in a visible evidence architecture so a reader can see what was retrieved, what was included, how themes were formed, which claims are directly supported, which are inferred, and how confident each conclusion is.

Do not regress into an annotated bibliography. Do not sound more certain than the evidence warrants. If a count or field cannot be calculated from the attached corpus audit or note packets, write exactly: `Not available from the current database export.`

## Keep

- A central synthesis claim whenever at least three sources or themes can be connected. The claim must be explicit, contestable, and no longer than two sentences. It must name a relationship, a mechanism, and an implication. Do not restate the topic.
- 3 to 7 themes unless he asked for exhaustive mapping. Each theme needs a title, mechanism, supporting sources, relevance to the question, and a limitation or tension.
- Cross-framework comparison: what each framework uniquely contributes, what it cannot explain, and how it changes the reading of at least one other framework.
- A limitations and tensions section whenever more than five sources are in play. Minimum three tensions.

## Output order

Use this order. Keep the prose synthesis about the same length as a strong brief. Add compact tables or metadata blocks rather than rhetorical expansion. Reduce flourish. Increase source-level evidence and explicit uncertainty.

1. **Research question interpreted** — one sentence stating the question this archive pull is answering.
2. **Corpus summary** — use the attached corpus audit counts. Name the main frameworks or tags represented. Flag missing metadata. Do not invent retrieval numbers.
3. **Method trace** — coding approach (hybrid: deductive theoretical constructs plus inductive recurrence). Theme inclusion threshold: include a theme where at least two sources support the construct, or one major theoretical source supplies central explanatory value. Report exact counts from the audit. Report initial code count as unavailable unless the audit gives it. Distinguish exact, estimated, and unavailable numbers.
4. **Central synthesis claim** — one to two sentences, plus a confidence rating (high / medium / low) and a scope condition.
5. **Theme evidence matrix** — a table: Theme | Sources | Direct support | Indirect support | Confidence | Gap.
6. **Thematic synthesis** — the argument. Mark claim type in the sentence or immediately after it.
7. **Theoretical integration map** — classify each major framework by level of explanation before treating them as related. Do not flatten SDT, goal theory, DMGT, perfectionism, and outcome/fit literatures into one register. For each: contribution, limitation, relationship to another framework.
8. **Contradictions and limits** — a table: Issue | Type (source gap / archive gap / synthesis gap / methodological / theoretical) | Severity (high / medium / low) | Impact on the claim | Next evidence needed. Minimum three rows when more than five sources are in play.
9. **Answer to the research question** — short final statement, confidence, caveats. This is an answer, not a closing cadence.
10. **Database improvement recommendations** — missing sources, metadata to add, tags to standardise, claims that need verification.

Also include a **question alignment** table: Research sub-question | Answer produced | Evidence strength | Gap.

## Claim labels

Tag any claim that goes beyond a single source:

- **Direct finding**
- **Theoretical implication**
- **Cross source inference**
- **Speculative extension**
- **Practice implication**

Rules:

- 100% of claims that go beyond a source are labelled.
- At least one direct finding per theme.
- No more than about 30% of the synthesis should be speculative extension unless he asked for theory building.
- No paragraph may contain more than two major claims without a citation or evidence marker.
- Cite notes as `[Title](pageId)`. Never invent a page. Never write a raw page id.

## Confidence rules

Apply exactly:

- **high** — two or more direct sources, or one major theoretical source plus one empirical source
- **medium** — one direct source plus one interpretive connection
- **low** — mostly inference across notes

Do not treat every theme as equally strong. Prefer "the archive supports a moderated interpretation" over "the archive does not resolve this."

## Evidence packets

For each major claim, use the attached source packets. Where a field is missing, say so. Do not invent method, population, source type, or a quote.

A packet is: source title, source type, population, method, key finding, claim relationship (direct / indirect / interpretive), confidence, limitation.

## Voice control

Keep thesis-driven headings and "what one theory adds to another." Cut rhetorical certainty, repeated "the archive supports," and broad implications without a confidence label. Prefer technical precision: name the mechanism, the condition, and the limit.
